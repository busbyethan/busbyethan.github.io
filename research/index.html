My research asks how AI can improve society and strengthen democracy, from helping people navigate disagreement to giving social scientists better ways to understand human behavior.

Four directions in my AI research

Simulating people

How closely can a language model reflect a human population?

The study proposes that language models can simulate specific human subpopulations when conditioned on detailed demographic backstories, while emphasizing the need to evaluate algorithmic fidelity carefully.

Out of One, Many: Using Language Models to Simulate Human Samples →

2023 · Political Analysis 31 (3): 337–351

Studying persuasion

What can AI help us learn about political persuasion?

Two survey experiments use generative AI to test theories of persuasion. AI-generated counterattitudinal messages changed some attitudes, but customization and interaction were not consistently more persuasive than a generic message.

Testing Theories of Political Persuasion Using Artificial Intelligence →

2025 · Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 122 (18): e2412815122

Gender, voice, and political influence.

Real-time conversations with AI agents let us separate voice-based gender cues from rhetoric and discussion topics. This study examines how these features shape political persuasion.

Looking under the hood.

Customization changed some messages without improving persuasion overall in the human study. The paper examines what models actually do when asked to tailor political arguments.

Improving conversations

Can AI help people have better political conversations?

Evidence-based AI suggestions improved reported conversation quality, democratic reciprocity, and tone without systematically changing the content of conversations or policy attitudes.

Evaluating models

How does alignment affect AI’s usefulness for social science?

The study compares aligned and unaligned open-source language models with human responses. It shows that alignment affects simulated attitudes in predictable ways and that model choice matters for social-science research.

Ongoing research at SAIL

Democratic skills.
Productive disagreement.

Can AI help people learn to disagree well? A central research program in our lab studies how AI can support the skills democratic conversation requires: participating, expressing a view, giving reasons, listening, and reconsidering one's own perspective.

We are developing ways to assess these skills, offer focused training, and provide practice with AI conversation partners. A key question is whether that practice carries over into conversations with other people. The program is in development; its effects are still being evaluated.
